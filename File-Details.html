File Details: What Goes in Each File

How Cowork reads your folder, which files matter most, and a copy-paste template for each one.

Files and templatesUpdated: 2026-06-19

Folders are the scaffolding; the files do the work. This page shows how Cowork reads your folder, then exactly what goes in each file, with templates you can copy. File names follow this guide's standard; common variants are noted.

How Cowork Reads Your Folder

Cowork treats your folder in three zones. Get the auto-read zone small and right, and every session starts cheap and consistent.

Solid arrows: read automatically every session. Dashed: loaded on demand when you scope a task.
Auto-Read

Your About/Context files and root CLAUDE.md / MEMORY.md. Pulled every session. Keep tiny, under ~2,000 words each.

On-Demand

Templates and project folders. Read only when you point Cowork to them. Can be large.

Write-Only

Outputs and Archive. Cowork drops finished work here; you do not hand-curate.

What Goes in Each File

Click a file to see its template. Copy, then fill in the brackets.

about-me.md

Who you are, what you do, who you serve, and how you want Claude to work with you. The most important file; keep it under ~2,000 words and update monthly.

# About Me

## Who I am
- 1-2 sentences: name, role, company, location.
- What makes your situation different from generic advice for your role.

## What I do day-to-day
- The 3-5 recurring activities that fill your week.
- Not a resume, a tour of your calendar.

## What I am working on now
- Top 1-3 current priorities. Update monthly.

## Who I serve
- Your customer, audience, or stakeholder, in their words.

## How I think
- Frameworks you use; what "rigorous" means to you.

## How I communicate
- Channels, tone, formatting preferences.

## How I want Claude to work with me
- Ask clarifying questions before drafting.
- Plan first, write second.
- Never delete files without confirmation.
- Prefer short bullets to long paragraphs.

## What is off-limits
- Topics, files, or tones Claude should refuse.

Keep it: Cowork re-reads it every session. Template basis: Mariah, Techlusion

writing-style.md (some guides call it voice-and-style.md)

Tone, audience, what to do and what to reject, plus links to a few writing samples. Anti-examples (what to reject) do more than extra positive samples.

# Writing Style

## Personality
A confident senior practitioner talking to a peer. Direct, specific,
plain language, never talking down.

## Audience
[One phrase from how your readers describe their own situation.]

## Do
- Lead with the answer; reasoning second.
- Use concrete numbers, names, dates.
- Prefer "you" over "the user".

## Don't
- "In today's fast-paced world..."
- "Unlock the power of..."
- Em dashes used as fake confidence.

## Samples
- Link 2-3 pieces of writing you are proud of.

Keep it: Four short sections is plenty. Alex Banks, Ruben Hassid

my-rules.md (some guides call it working-rules.md)

The behavioral guardrails: when to ask, when to plan, how to handle files, and the quality bar.

# Working Rules

## Before acting
- ASK if intent is under 90 percent clear.
- PLAN first when a task touches more than 3 files. Show the plan, wait.
- READ before MODIFY: quote the current state before changing it.

## File handling
- NEVER delete files without explicit confirmation.
- ALWAYS save final outputs to the Outputs folder, date-prefixed.
- NEVER write inside the About/Context folder without permission.

## Quality bar
- "Good" means correct, concise, defensible.
- A 3-bullet answer beats a 3-paragraph answer.
- If you do not know, say "I don't know". Do not invent.

Keep it: Be specific about what Claude must NEVER do. Jeff Su

CLAUDE.md

The workspace map and read-order, kept terse. Anti-pattern: treating it like documentation with long paragraphs.

# CLAUDE.md, workspace manual

## Read order every session
1. About/about-me.md
2. About/writing-style.md
3. About/my-rules.md
4. Whatever folder I scope you to.

## Folder map
- About/      auto-read, tiny, do not modify without permission
- Templates/  reference only, use when I ask
- Projects/   active work, one folder per project
- Outputs/    write finished work here, always date-prefix
- Archive/    move completed projects here

## Hard rules
- See About/my-rules.md.

Keep it: A map plus hard rules, not prose. The AI Corner

MEMORY.md

A notepad Cowork writes to across sessions: things learned, decisions made, open questions. You skim and prune.

# MEMORY.md

## Things learned about how I work
- (Claude appends here over time)

## Decisions made
- 2026-05-12: settled on the H2-every-250-words blog format.

## Open questions
- (Claude should ask me when one of these comes up)

Keep it: Cowork writes here; you curate. Jeff Su

A Note on File Names
Naming. This guide uses about-me.md, writing-style.md, and my-rules.md. Other popular guides use voice-and-style.md and working-rules.md for the last two; the contents are the same. Pick one set and stay consistent. See Recommendations for the full naming standard.
